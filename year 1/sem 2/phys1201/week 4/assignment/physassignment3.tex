\section{Part 1}

We're given $V(x) = B\left(1 - 4\frac{x^2}{D^2}\right)$

We know that force is given by $F = -\frac{dV}{dx}$

taking derivative:
\[
\frac{dV}{dx} = \frac{d}{dx} \left[ B - \frac{4Bx^2}{D^2} \right] = -\frac{8Bx}{D^2}
\]

therefore, force is:
\[
F = - \left(-\frac{8Bx}{D^2}\right) = \frac{8Bx}{D^2}
\]

Applying Newton's second law of motion, $F=ma \implies F=m\ddot{x}$:
\[
m\ddot{x} = \frac{8Bx}{D^2} \implies \ddot{x} = \left(\frac{8B}{mD^2}\right)x
\]

comparing to $\ddot{x} = \Omega^2 x$,
\[
\Omega^2 = \frac{8B}{mD^2}
\]
\[
\Omega = \sqrt{\frac{8B}{mD^2}}
\]

\section{Part 3}

kinetic energy is:
\[
\frac{1}{2}m\dot{x}^2
\]

The action $S$ over a time interval from $0$ to $T$ is the integral of the Lagrangian $L = T - V$:
\[
S = \int_{0}^{T} \left( \frac{1}{2}m\dot{x}^2 - V(x) \right) dt
\]

Differentiating $x(t) = -\frac{D}{2}\cos\left(\frac{\pi t}{T}\right)$ with respect to time $t$:
\[
\dot{x}(t) = \frac{D\pi}{2T} \sin\left(\frac{\pi t}{T}\right)
\]

Kinetic Energy:
\[
\frac{1}{2}m\dot{x}^2 = \frac{1}{2}m \left( \frac{D\pi}{2T} \sin\left(\frac{\pi t}{T}\right) \right)^2 = \frac{m D^2 \pi^2}{8 T^2} \sin^2\left(\frac{\pi t}{T}\right)
\]
Potential Energy:

substituting $x(t) = -\frac{D}{2}\cos\left(\frac{\pi t}{T}\right)$ into $V(x)$:
\[
V(t) = B \left(1 - \frac{4}{D^2} \left[ -\frac{D}{2}\cos\left(\frac{\pi t}{T}\right) \right]^2\right) = B \left(1 - \cos^2\left(\frac{\pi t}{T}\right)\right) = B \sin^2\left(\frac{\pi t}{T}\right)
\]
integrating:
\[
S = \int_{0}^{T} \left[ \frac{m D^2 \pi^2}{8 T^2} \sin^2\left(\frac{\pi t}{T}\right) - B \sin^2\left(\frac{\pi t}{T}\right) \right] dt
\]
\[S = \left( \frac{m D^2 \pi^2}{8 T^2} - B \right) \int_{0}^{T} \sin^2\left(\frac{\pi t}{T}\right) dt
\]
using the formula $\int_{0}^{T} \sin^2\left(\frac{\pi t}{T}\right)dt = \frac{T}{2}$:
\[
S = \left( \frac{m D^2 \pi^2}{8 T^2} - B \right) \frac{T}{2} = \frac{m D^2 \pi^2}{16 T} - \frac{B T}{2}
\]
Matching $S = \frac{C_1}{T} - C_2 T$:
\[
C_1 = \frac{m D^2 \pi^2}{16}, \quad C_2 = \frac{B}{2}
\]

\section {Part 4}

the most probable tunnelling time $T_0$ requires the action to be stationary with respect to $T$:
\[
\frac{dS}{dT}\Big|_{T=T_0} = 0
\]

Differentiating $S$ with respect to $T$:
\[
\frac{dS}{dT} = -\frac{C_1}{T^2} - C_2
\]

at $T = T_0$,
\[
-\frac{C_1}{T_0^2} - C_2 = 0 \implies T_0^2 = -\frac{C_1}{C_2}
\]

$T_0^2$ is negative meaning $T_0$ must be imaginary:
\[
T_0 = \pm i \sqrt{\frac{C_1}{C_2}}
\]
where $C_3 = \sqrt{\frac{C_1}{C_2}}$ 

Substituting $C_1 = \frac{m D^2 \pi^2}{16}$ and $C_2 = \frac{B}{2}$ into $C_3$:
\[
C_3 = \sqrt{\frac{\frac{m D^2 \pi^2}{16}}{\frac{B}{2}}} = \sqrt{\frac{2m D^2 \pi^2}{16 B}} = \frac{D\pi}{4}\sqrt{\frac{m}{2B}}
\]

\section{Part 5}

Substituting $T = -iC_3$ into $S = \frac{C_1}{T} - C_2 T$ ($C_1 = C_2 C_3^2$):
\[
S = \frac{C_2 C_3^2}{-i C_3} - C_2(-i C_3)
\]
\[
S = i C_2 C_3 + i C_2 C_3 = 2i C_2 C_3
\]

\section{Part 6}

substituting $C_2 = \frac{B}{2}$ and $C_3 = \frac{D\pi}{4}\sqrt{\frac{m}{2B}}$ into our expression for $S$:
\[
S = 2i \left(\frac{B}{2}\right) \left(\frac{D\pi}{4}\sqrt{\frac{m}{2B}}\right)
\]
\[
S = i \frac{B D \pi}{4} \sqrt{\frac{m}{2B}} = i \frac{\pi D}{4} \sqrt{\frac{mB}{2}}
\]

Assuming that only the path with tunnelling time $T_0$ contributes, the tunnelling probability $P$ is proportional to $e^{iS/\hbar}$
\[
P \propto e^{i \frac{\pi D}{4} \sqrt{\frac{mB}{2}}}
\]
let $\alpha$ incorporate the collected constants ($\frac{\pi}{4\hbar}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$). we get:
\[
P \propto e^{- \alpha D\sqrt{mB}}
\]
