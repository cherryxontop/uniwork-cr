\section{Question 1}

two masses, each of mass $m$, are connected by a spring with spring constant $k$. 

\subsection*{(a)} 
since the two masses are identical, the centre of mass is in the middle of the spring. 
if one mass moves a distance $x$ away from the centre of mass, the other mass moves a 
distance $x$ in the opposite direction. 

total change in the length of the spring is 
\[
x+x=2x
\]

from the hint, if the length of a spring is halved, its effective spring constant doubles. 
so, each half of the spring has spring constant 
\[
k_{\text{half}}=2k
\]

for one mass, the restoring force is $F=-2kx$

using Newton's second law, 
\[
F=m\frac{d^2x}{dt^2}
\]
\[
m\frac{d^2x}{dt^2}=-2kx
\]

rearranging, 
\[
\frac{d^2x}{dt^2}+\frac{2k}{m}x=0
\]

rhe standard equation for simple harmonic motion is 
\[
\frac{d^2x}{dt^2}+\omega^2x=0
\]

comparing the two equations gives
\[ \omega^2=\frac{2k}{m}
\]

therefore
\[
\omega=\sqrt{\frac{2k}{m}}
\]

since $\omega=2\pi f$, we have 
\[
2\pi f=\sqrt{\frac{2k}{m}}
\]

therefore, the resonant frequency is
\[
\boxed{ f=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{\frac{2k}{m}} }
\] 

\subsection*{(b)}
we are given $m=1\text{ AMU}$ and $f=125\text{ THz}$. Converting the mass into SI units,
\[
m=1.66054\times10^{-27}\text{ kg}
\]
\[
f=125\times10^{12}\text{ Hz} =1.25\times10^{14}\text{ Hz}
\]

from part (a),
\[
f=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{\frac{2k}{m}}
\]

multiplying both sides by $2\pi$
\[
2\pi f=\sqrt{\frac{2k}{m}}
\]

squaring both sides,
\[
4\pi^2f^2=\frac{2k}{m}
\]
\[
k=2\pi^2f^2m
\]

substituting the values,
\[
k = 2\pi^2 (1.25\times10^{14})^2 (1.66054\times10^{-27})
\]
\[
k\approx510.6\text{ N/m}
\]
hence, 
\[
\boxed{k\approx5.11\times10^2\text{ N/m} \text{ or } k\approx511\text{ N/m}}
\]

\section{Question 2}

we are given:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $r=0.05\text{kg/s}$
    \item $x_0=10\text{cm} = 0.1\text{m}$
    \item $v_0=0$
    \item $k=5\text{N/m}$
    \item $m=1\text{kg}$
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{(a)}

the equation for a damped mass-spring system is

\[
m\frac{d^2x}{dt^2} + r\frac{dx}{dt} + kx=0
\]

substituting the given values,

\[
\frac{d^2x}{dt^2} + 0.05\frac{dx}{dt} + 5x=0
\]

the standard form is

\[
x''+2\gamma x'+\omega_0^2x=0
\]

comparing coefficients,
\[
2\gamma=0.05
\]
\[
\gamma=0.025\text{ s}^{-1}
\]

the undamped angular frequency is
\[
\omega_0=\sqrt{\frac{k}{m}} = \sqrt{\frac{5}{1}} = \sqrt{5} \approx2.236\text{ rad/s}
\]

the damped angular frequency is
\[
\omega_d=\sqrt{\omega_0^2-\gamma^2}
\]
\[
\omega_d=\sqrt{5-(0.025)^2}\]
\[
\omega_d \approx2.23593\text{ rad/s}
\]

for an underdamped oscillator, the general solution is
\[
x(t)=e^{-\gamma t}\left[A\cos(\omega_dt)+B\sin(\omega_dt)\right]
\]

therefore,
\[
x(t)=e^{-0.025t}\left[A\cos(2.23593t)+B\sin(2.23593t)\right]
\]

using the initial displacement,
\[
x(0)=0.10
\]

at $t=0$,

\[
0.1=A\cos(0)+B\sin(0)
\]

since $\cos(0)=1 \text{ and } \sin(0)=0$, we get

\[
A=0.10
\]

therefore,

\[
x(t) = e^{-0.025t}\left[0.10\cos(2.23593t)+B\sin(2.23593t)\right]
\]

differentiating to apply the initial velocity condition,

\[
v(t)=-\gamma e^{-\gamma t}\left[A\cos(\omega_dt)+B\sin(\omega_dt)\right]+e^{-\gamma t}\left[-A\omega_d\sin(\omega_dt)+B\omega_d\cos(\omega_dt)\right]
\]

at $t=0$,

\[
v(0)=-\gamma A+B\omega_d
\]

since $v(0)=0$,

\[
0=-\gamma A+B\omega_d
\]
\[
B=\frac{\gamma A}{\omega_d}
\]

substituting the values,

\[
B=\frac{(0.025)(0.10)}{2.23593}
\]
\[
B\approx0.001119
\]

therefore, the position as a function of time is

\[
\boxed{
x(t)=e^{-0.025t}\left[0.10\cos(2.23593t)+0.001119\sin(2.23593t)\right]\text{ m}}
\]

\subsection*{(b)}

initially, the mass is displaced by $x_0=0.10\text{ m}$ and has zero velocity.

therefore, the initial energy is entirely spring potential energy

\[
E_0=\frac{1}{2}kx_0^2.
\]

substituting,

\[
E_0=\frac{1}{2}(5)(0.10)^2
\]

thus,

\[
E_0=\frac{1}{2}(5)(0.01)
\]

and

\[
E_0=0.025\text{ J}.
\]

the damped angular frequency is

\[
\omega_d=2.23593\text{ rad/s}
\]

therefore, the oscillation period is

\[
T=\frac{2\pi}{\omega_d}.
\]

substituting,

\[
T=\frac{2\pi}{2.23593}\approx2.8101\text{ s}
\]

after 10 periods,

\[
t=10T
\]

so

\[
t=10(2.8101)\approx28.101\text{ s}
\]

the amplitude decays according to

\[
A(t)=A_0e^{-\gamma t}
\]

since the energy is proportional to the square of the amplitude,

\[
E(t)=E_0e^{-2\gamma t}
\]

therefore,

\[
E(t)=0.025e^{-2(0.025)(28.101)}
\]

calculating the exponent,

\[
2(0.025)(28.101)=1.40505
\]

thus,

\[
E(t)=0.025e^{-1.40505}.
\]

this gives

\[
E(t)\approx0.006134\text{ J}
\]

therefore, the energy lost is

\[
\Delta E=E_0-E(t)
\]

hence,

\[
\Delta E=0.025-0.006134
\]

\[
\boxed{\Delta E\approx0.01887\text{ J}}
\]

\section{Question 3}

the pipe is closed at one end and open at the other. at the closed end there is a displacement node, 
while at the open end there is a displacement antinode. for the fundamental mode, the pipe contains one quarter of a wavelength:

\[
L=\frac{\lambda_1}{4}
\]
\[
\lambda_1=4L
\]

using $f=\frac{c}{\lambda}$,

\[
f_1=\frac{c}{4L}
\]

for higher modes, only odd harmonics are allowed. therefore,

\[
n=1,3,5,7,\ldots
\]
\[
L=\frac{n\lambda_n}{4}
\]
\[
4L=n\lambda_n,
\]
\[
\lambda_n=\frac{4L}{n}
\]

we have,

\[
f_n
=
\frac{c}{\lambda_n}
\]

substituting for $\lambda_n$,

\[
f_n
=
\frac{c}{4L/n}
\]

hence,

\[
\boxed{
    f_n=\frac{nc}{4L},\qquad n=1,3,5,7,\ldots
    }
\]

\section{Question 4}

we have,

\begin{itemize}
    \item speed of dolphin, $v_s = 40\text{ km/h} = 40\left(\frac{1000}{3600}\right) = 11.111\text{ m/s}$
    \item frequency produced by sound, $f_s = 1000\text{ Hz}$
\end{itemize}

the Doppler-shifted frequency is

\[
f_{\text{heard}}=f_s\frac{v}{v-v_s},
\]

where $v$ is the speed of sound in the medium.

\subsection*{(a)}

the speed of sound in water is approximately $v=1500\text{ m/s}$. therefore,

\[
f_{\text{heard}}=1000\left(\frac{1500}{1500-11.11}\right)
\]
\[
f_{\text{heard}}\approx1007.5\text{ Hz}
\]

therefore,

\[
\boxed{f_{\text{heard}}\approx1008\text{ Hz}}
\]


\subsection*{(b)}

taking the speed of sound in air to be approximately $v=343\text{ m/s}$ and substituting in $f_{\text{heard}}=f_s\frac{v}{v-v_s}$,

\[
f_{\text{heard}}=1000\left(\frac{343}{343-11.11}\right)
\]
\[
f_{\text{heard}}\approx1033.5\text{ Hz}
\]

therefore,

\[
\boxed{f_{\text{heard}}\approx1034\text{ Hz}}
\]
